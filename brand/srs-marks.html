Solare Source identity

Three marks for SRS.

All monochrome, all flat, all built on a 40 unit grid with no curve, no gradient and no shadow. Each one survives at 16px and reverses without modification. Pick one and it becomes the mark; the other two get retired.

Direction 01

Laminate

Three equal bars under a constant shear. It is a panel coming apart into clean layers, which is the entire business, and at a glance it resolves into an S. The most literal of the three and the one that explains itself fastest.

Direction 02

Modular S

An S drawn with no curve at all, from five rectangles on one stroke width. It is the most institutional of the three and the most typographic, which means it sits beside the wordmark without competing with it. Reads as a letter first, a structure second.

Direction 03

Cell

A sixteen cell panel with its top row lifted clear. The densest of the three and the most specific to solar, because nothing else looks like a cell matrix. It pays for that specificity at small sizes, where the row separation is the first thing to go.

Direction 04

Aperture

One ring, one chevron dropped inside it. The ring is the sun and the aperture that takes it in; the chevron is the direction that energy travels, down and into the building. The only direction here built on a curve, which is what makes it read as a symbol rather than a diagram, and the only one that holds its shape at favicon size without losing a detail.

However it is used, these hold.

Monochrome onlyInk #1E1F2B or surface #EFEFEF. The mark is never tinted, never two colour, never placed on a gradient.
Clear spaceOne bar height on every side, taken from the mark's own stroke. Nothing enters that margin, including the wordmark.
Minimum size16px for the mark alone. 96px wide for the full lockup, below which the wordmark loses its counters.
WordmarkAlliance No 1 Medium, tracking -0.03em, set to the mark's cap height. Archivo Semibold is the fallback where Alliance is not licensed.
NeverNo outline version, no rotation, no drop shadow, no rounding of the corners, no redrawing the bars at a different shear.
